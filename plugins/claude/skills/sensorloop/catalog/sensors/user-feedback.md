This sensor answers what no other can: whether users want what was built. It is also the slowest signal of all: it
arrives over weeks.

## What it sees

Whether a change helps the people it was made for: what they complain about, what they ask for, what they use and what
they leave alone. No program or model can answer that on their behalf.

It costs time and attention: a trend takes weeks of reports, and a person reads and groups the items, or checks the
grouping a model proposed. It hears only from users who write in or whose behaviour is measured, and those who write
in are not a fair sample of all users.

## Reading the signal

- **A topic with its count**: how many users raised it, and through which channel. The count, not the tone, sets its
  weight.
- **A single loud report**: not a trend. It becomes one only if the count grows.
- **An experiment result**: whether users valued the change, judged by what they did rather than what they said.

A drop in reports can mean the channel broke (a form that fails, a support queue that moved) rather than that users
are happier. Check that the channel still delivers before reading silence as satisfaction.
